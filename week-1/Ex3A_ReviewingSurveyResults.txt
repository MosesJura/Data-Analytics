SURVEY QUESTION 1: What do you envisage yourself doing 5 minutes before class starts each day, to ensure that you are ready to start on time?
----------------------------------------

1. What conclusion can you reach about the responses to this question?

Most respondents focus on preparation and readiness. Common themes include:
- Reviewing notes or previous material
- Logging in early and checking equipment
- Getting organized (coffee, water, workspace)
- Being mentally ready (recentering, getting focused)
- Ensuring they are on time

The responses show that students value a consistent routine before class.
A few respondents mentioned being prepared 30 minutes early, which suggests
some students prefer a longer buffer than just 5 minutes.

2. Is the default summary or visualization helpful? How could it be improved?

The default summary for a written-response question is usually just a list
of text answers. This is not very helpful for finding patterns because the
responses are all different lengths and wording.

Improvements:
- Add word cloud or keyword frequency chart
- Group similar answers into themes (e.g., "review notes," "log in early,"
  "get coffee/water," "mental preparation")
- Use multiple-choice or checkbox options for common activities, plus an
  "Other" text box for unique answers

3. For questions with an "Other" option, where can you find the actual text
   of those responses? Do you notice anything tricky?

This question is open-ended and does not have an "Other" option. If it did,
the actual text would appear in the downloadable Excel file, usually in a
column next to the question. The tricky part is that "Other" responses are
often inconsistent — different spellings, phrasing, and levels of detail —
making them hard to group or count automatically.

4. For questions calling for a written response, what challenges do you
   notice about finding trends or patterns in the answers?

Challenges:
- Responses are free text, so wording varies even when the idea is the same
- Some answers are fragments (e.g., "Get ready for class")
- Some answers are long and detailed, others are very short
- No standard categories to count
- Requires manual reading and tagging to find themes
- Hard to visualize without extra processing

5. Now that you can see the responses, how might you recommend rephrasing
   this question or the response options in a future survey?

Recommendation:
Instead of a single open-text question, use a multiple-choice or
multi-select question with common options, plus an "Other" text box.

Example:
"What do you do in the 5 minutes before class? (Select all that apply)"
- Review notes
- Log in to class early
- Check equipment (monitor, keyboard, mouse)
- Get coffee/water/snack
- Silence phone
- Mentally prepare/focus
- Other: __________

This would make responses easier to compare, count, and visualize.

ADDITIONAL QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------

6. Is there anything that surprised you about the survey responses?

Yes. Many respondents mentioned being prepared much earlier than 5 minutes
(e.g., 30 minutes before class). Also, several responses focused on
emotional or mental readiness ("recentering," "getting myself together"),
not just logistical tasks. This suggests the question is about more than
just physical preparation.

7. Looking at the responses to questions 1 and 5, are there any ideas you
   might try to include in your morning routine, or any questions that you
   are also curious about?

Ideas to try:
- Review yesterday's notes for 5 minutes before class
- Log in 5-10 minutes early to test audio/video
- Keep water and coffee ready the night before
- Put phone face down and out of reach
- Write one goal for the day before class starts

New questions I am curious about:
- How many students actually follow a consistent morning routine?
- Does preparing earlier than 5 minutes lead to better focus?
- What barriers prevent students from being ready on time?

8. Pretend you are the instructor. Is there anything you can learn from this
   survey to help in how you approach teaching this class?

Yes:
- Students value preparation and routine. The instructor could start each
  class with a 1-minute "settle in" moment to help students transition.
- Some students arrive 30 minutes early, while others aim for 5 minutes.
  The instructor could share a recommended pre-class checklist.
- Since many students mentioned reviewing notes, the instructor could
  provide a quick recap or warm-up question at the start of class.
- The instructor could also model good habits by starting on time and
  making the first 5 minutes predictable and calm.

9. Again, pretend you are the instructor. Do you think it was helpful or
   not helpful to make this survey anonymous? Why or why not?

Pros of anonymity:
- Students may be more honest about struggles (e.g., time management,
  not having equipment).
- Reduces pressure to give "correct" or impressive answers.
- Encourages participation.

Cons of anonymity:
- Cannot follow up with individual students who need help.
- Cannot identify who may need a loaner monitor or keyboard.
- Harder to build accountability or personalized support.

Overall: For this survey, anonymity was helpful because it increased
honesty. But if the instructor wants to offer equipment loans or follow-up
support, a non-anonymous or optionally anonymous version would be better.

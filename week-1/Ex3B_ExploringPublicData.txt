MODULE 3 - EXERCISE 3.B: EXPLORING PUBLIC DATA
Filled-In Answers

1. Start by reviewing the data.gov home page. What is this?

Answer: Data.gov is the central clearinghouse for U.S. government open data.
It is managed by the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA). The site
provides public access to datasets from federal, state, local, and tribal
government agencies. Users can search, browse, and download data on topics
such as health, climate, education, agriculture, transportation, and more.
It is designed to promote transparency, innovation, and data-driven
decision-making.

2. Go to Most Viewed Datasets. How many datasets are listed?

Answer: The number changes over time, but typically there are more than 10
datasets listed on the "Most Viewed Datasets" page. 


3. Find the Topic Category for Human Health. Review the range of data
   sources available for this topic. Why do you think these were tagged
   with the Human Health category?

Answer: The Human Health category includes datasets from agencies such as:
- National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, Department of Commerce
- National Institutes of Health (NIH)
- Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)
- Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS)

These datasets are tagged with "Human Health" because they relate to:
- Disease tracking and prevention
- Hospital and provider information
- Nutrition and food safety
- Drug and medical device data
- Health insurance and payment data
- Public health statistics

They help researchers, policymakers, and the public understand health
trends, evaluate programs, and make informed decisions.

4. Go to the Food Price Outlook. Review the catalog page for this dataset.
   How can you navigate to additional datasets from the USDA?

Answer: On the Food Price Outlook catalog page, you can navigate to
additional USDA datasets by:
- Clicking on the "USDA" organization link near the top of the page
- Using the "Related" or "Associated" datasets section if available
- Clicking on the "Visit Page" button to go to the USDA Economic Research
  Service (ERS) website, where more datasets are listed
- Searching Data.gov for "USDA" to see all datasets from that organization

5. Go back to the data.gov home page. This time, click on Datasets by
   Organization.

Answer: The "Datasets by Organization" page lists all government
organizations that have published datasets on Data.gov. You can browse
alphabetically or search for a specific agency. Examples include:
- Department of Agriculture (USDA)
- Department of Commerce
- Department of Education
- Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
- National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)

6. Try searching for your city, county, and state. What organizations are
   returned?
- City of New Castle
- New castle County
- State of Delaware
- Federal agencies with local data (Department of Interior)

This shows that public data exists at multiple levels of government:
federal, state, county, and city.

7. Search for USDA, and click into the result. Within the USDA datasets,
   search for "food composition." Notice that the top three results are
   all datasets concerning nutrition information. Try searching the USDA
   page for "nutrition." How does that affect your results?

Answer: Searching for "food composition" returns datasets like:
- FoodData Central
- USDA National Nutrient Database
- Nutrient Database for Standard Reference

Searching for "nutrition" returns broader results:
- Food and Nutrition Service data
- Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) data
- School lunch and child nutrition program data
- Dietary guidelines and nutrition research

Key insight: Different search terms return different results. "Food
composition" is more specific to nutrient content, while "nutrition" is
broader and includes food assistance programs and policy data.

a) One tricky thing about working with data is that sometimes the
   terminology used by a data source is not obvious. You may need to dig
   around and be creative to find what you're looking for.

Answer: This is true. Government agencies use specific jargon and
classification systems. For example:
- "Food composition" vs. "nutrition" vs. "dietary intake"
- "Provider" vs. "physician" vs. "clinician"
- "Beneficiary" vs. "recipient" vs. "participant"

Being creative with search terms and exploring related datasets is an
important data discovery skill.

8. Go back to the results for food composition, and click on FoodData
   Central. Review the catalog page for this data source, then click on
   the button for Visit Page. Take some time to explore the FoodData
   Central website.

Answer: FoodData Central is maintained by the USDA Agricultural Research
Service (ARS). It provides detailed information about the nutrient
composition of foods. The catalog page includes:
- Description of the dataset
- Metadata (last updated, publisher, contact)
- Download links
- Related datasets

On the FoodData Central website, you can:
- Search for foods by name
- View nutrient profiles (calories, protein, fat, vitamins, minerals)
- Download data in CSV or JSON format
- Access different data types (Foundation Foods, SR Legacy, Branded Foods)

a) Try searching for butter, or any of your favorite foods. What kind of
   results do you get? What happens when you click on a result?

Answer: Searching for "butter" returns multiple results:
- Butter, salted
- Butter, unsalted
- Butter, whipped
- Butter, light
- Butter Creamy

Clicking on a result shows:
- Full nutrient profile per 100g
- Calories, fat, protein, carbohydrates
- Vitamins and minerals
- Data source and publication date
- Links to related foods

b) Go back to the home page. Find Foundation Foods, and click on
   Downloads. What kind of data can you find on this page? How big are
   these files? Where can you find more information on the data types
   available?

Answer: Foundation Foods downloads include:
- CSV files with nutrient data
- JSON files with structured data
- Documentation and data dictionaries

File sizes vary but can range from a few MB to hundreds of MB for full
datasets.

More information on data types can be found in:
- The "Documentation" section
- The "Data Types" page
- The "Download Data" page with descriptions of each dataset

Data types include:
- Foundation Foods: Basic foods with detailed nutrient analysis
- SR Legacy: Older nutrient database
- Branded Foods: Packaged food products with label data
- Survey Foods: Foods reported in national surveys

========================================
KEY TAKEAWAYS
========================================

- Data.gov is the central clearinghouse for U.S. government open data.
- Public data exists at federal, state, county, and city levels.
- Different search terms return different results; be creative and
  persistent.
- Government agencies use specific terminology that may not be obvious.
- FoodData Central is a rich source of nutrition data with multiple
  download formats.
- Always review the catalog page and metadata before using a dataset.
- File sizes can be large; check before downloading.
- Documentation is essential for understanding data types and structure.
